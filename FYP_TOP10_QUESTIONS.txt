QUESTION 1
"What is an Expert System, and how is it used in your project?"
------------------------------------------------------------------------

AN EXPERT SYSTEM is a rule-based computer program that mimics the
decision-making ability of a human expert in a specific domain.
Instead of learning from data (like a neural network), it follows
a fixed set of IF-THEN rules defined by the programmer.

IN TASMIQAI:
Our Expert System compares a student's Quran recitation
transcription against the expected Quran verse text.

It applies these rules:
  - IF word matches expected word → CORRECT
  - IF word is 65%+ similar to expected → PRONUNCIATION ISSUE
  - IF word is 35-64% similar → INCORRECT WORD
  - IF expected word is completely missing → SKIPPED
  - IF makhraj-sensitive letter appears in an error position
    → Add Makhraj guidance tip

It then calculates scores and generates feedback.

IMPORTANT: The Expert System is our own code (Python, tasmiq_app.py).
It is NOT the Gemini AI. Gemini only does speech recognition.
Our Expert System does the decision-making.

QUESTION 2
"What does the AI model do in your system?"
------------------------------------------------------------------------

We use Google's Gemini Flash model for Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR).

WHAT GEMINI DOES:
  - Receives the student's audio recording
  - Transcribes it into Arabic text
  - We guide it with the expected verse as context in our prompt

WHAT GEMINI DOES NOT DO:
  - Gemini does NOT evaluate the recitation
  - Gemini does NOT score the student
  - Gemini does NOT know what "correct" means in our system
  - We do NOT know how Gemini processes the audio internally
    (it is a black box neural network)

AFTER GEMINI:
Our Expert System takes the transcription text and applies our
rules to score it.

CORRECT ARCHITECTURE STATEMENT:
"Gemini Flash is responsible for speech recognition.
Our Expert System is responsible for rule-based evaluation.
These are two separate components."

QUESTION 3
"How is the score calculated?"
------------------------------------------------------------------------

The overall score is a WEIGHTED AVERAGE of four components:

  Overall = Memorization × 0.45
          + Pronunciation × 0.30
          + Tajweed × 0.15
          + Fluency × 0.10

MEMORIZATION (45%):
  = (weighted correct words / total expected words) × 100
  Each correct word = 1.0 point
  Each pronunciation issue = 0.75 points
  Each incorrect word = 0.2 points
  Each skipped word = 0 points

PRONUNCIATION (30%):
  = 100 − (pronunciation issues / total words × 35)
  Capped: cannot exceed Memorization + 12 points

TAJWEED (15%):
  = 100 − (number of makhraj errors × 6)
  Capped: cannot exceed Memorization + 15 points
  (Based on makhraj-sensitive Arabic letters: ق ض ط ظ ث ذ ه ح خ غ ص)

FLUENCY (10%):
  = Proportion of speech frames in the audio × 110
  Higher continuous speech ratio → higher fluency

PASS THRESHOLD:
  Score ≥ 70% → PASS
  Score < 70% → NEEDS MORE PRACTICE

QUESTION 4
"How does the 70% threshold work?"
------------------------------------------------------------------------

The 70% threshold is used in two ways:

WAY 1 — Visual feedback to student:
  In TasmiqModeScreen and HistoryScreen:
    IF score >= 70 → show green "PASS" badge
    IF score < 70  → show amber "NEEDS MORE PRACTICE" badge

WAY 2 — Unlock condition for Official Assessment:
  In TasmiqPrepScreen, the student can only select
  "Official Assessment" if they have completed at least
  ONE AI Practice session for that Surah.

IMPORTANT LIMITATION:
  The current code checks IF the student has done at least
  one AI Practice (any score), NOT whether they scored ≥ 70%.
  A student who scored 20% in AI Practice can technically
  access Official Assessment.

  This is a known gap between the intended design (must score ≥70%)
  and the current implementation (must have attempted at least once).

QUESTION 5
"How does the teacher assess a student's recitation?"
------------------------------------------------------------------------

STEP 1: Student submits an Official Assessment.
  - is_exercise = false
  - status = 'pending', reviewed = false

STEP 2: Teacher opens the Teacher Portal.
  - RecitationReview.jsx or TasmiqWorkspace.jsx
  - Pending queue: SELECT * FROM recitations WHERE reviewed = false

STEP 3: Teacher selects the student's recitation.
  - Sees: Student name, Surah, Ayah, AI-generated score breakdown,
    transcription, word alignment, audio recording

STEP 4: Teacher plays the audio recording.

STEP 5: Teacher selects PASS or REPEAT.
  - REPEAT requires written feedback (enforced in code)

STEP 6: Database updated:
  - recitations.status → 'approved' (PASS) or 'repeat' (REPEAT)
  - recitations.teacher_id → teacher's UUID
  - recitations.teacher_status → 'PASS' or 'REPEAT'
  - recitations.reviewed → true

STEP 7: Notification created for the student:
  - notifications.user_id = student UUID (from recitations.user_id)
  - NOT based on student name — based on UUID

STEP 8: Student receives push notification via Supabase Realtime.
  - No need to refresh or log out/in
  - Notification deep-links to the evaluation screen

QUESTION 6
"What is Murajaah and how is it different from Tasmiq?"
------------------------------------------------------------------------

MURAJAAH (Revision):
  - Purpose: Self-revision and memorization practice
  - No recording
  - No AI analysis
  - No scoring
  - No teacher involvement
  - Student reveals/hides ayahs to test their own memory
  - Can listen to reference audio at normal or slow speed
  - Progress saved to murajaah_sessions table
  - Completion when all ayahs reviewed at least once

TASMIQ (Assessment):
  - Purpose: Formal recitation assessment
  - Student records audio
  - AI (Gemini) transcribes the audio
  - Expert System scores the transcription
  - Score saved to recitations table
  - AI Practice: immediate auto-scored result
  - Official Assessment: submitted to teacher for formal review

KEY DIFFERENCE:
  Murajaah is for learning. Tasmiq is for assessment.
  Murajaah is like studying. Tasmiq is like taking the test.

QUESTION 7
"How does the notification system work?"
------------------------------------------------------------------------

The notification system uses Supabase Realtime.

WHEN TEACHER SUBMITS EVALUATION:
  Teacher portal inserts a row into the notifications table:
    user_id = student UUID    ← identifies the correct student
    type = 'TEACHER_TASMIQ_EVALUATION'
    title = 'Teacher Assessment Completed' (PASS)
           OR 'Teacher Requested Re-recording' (REPEAT)
    recitation_id = the specific recitation UUID (for deep-link)

STUDENT SIDE (Real-time):
  The student's mobile app subscribes to a Supabase Realtime channel:
    Channel: "notifications:{studentId}"
    Event: INSERT on notifications table WHERE user_id = studentId
  
  When the teacher inserts the notification, Supabase pushes it
  to the subscribed mobile app immediately.
  
  The unread count badge on the bell icon updates instantly.
  
  Student taps the notification → navigates to TeacherEvaluationScreen.

SECURITY:
  - All notification queries filter by user_id
  - markAsRead() also verifies user_id (cross-student read impossible)
  - Real-time channel is scoped to the specific student's UUID

QUESTION 8
"What happens if the AI server (backend) is offline?"
------------------------------------------------------------------------

There are two scenarios:

SCENARIO 1: Gemini API unavailable (no API key or Gemini fails)
  → Acoustic Signal Analysis fallback automatically activates
  → Uses librosa to analyze audio signal properties:
      - Speech/silence ratio
      - Zero-crossing rate (articulation)
      - Energy variance (smoothness)
      - Duration vs expected
  → Produces realistic scores from acoustic features
  → Student sees a result — no crash, no error message
  → Source field in result: "acoustic" (vs "gemini")

SCENARIO 2: Entire FastAPI backend unreachable
  → Mobile app shows alert: "Analysis Failed — Could not reach the AI Server"
  → Student cannot submit to AI in that session
  → The app does NOT crash
  → Student can still browse History, Profile, Murajaah (these use Supabase directly)

IMPORTANT:
  The backend runs separately from the database.
  Supabase database operations (load history, notifications, profile)
  still work even if the FastAPI backend is offline.

QUESTION 9
"How are previous recitation attempts preserved?"
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every recitation submission is an INSERT, never an UPDATE.

CODE PROOF (recitationService.js → saveRecitationResult):
  supabase.from('recitations').insert([insertPayload]).select().single()

Each submission creates a NEW row with:
  - A new UUID
  - A timestamp (submitted_at, recorded_at)
  - An attempt_number (COUNT of previous exercises + 1)
  - A session_id (UUID grouping multi-ayah beginner sessions)

RESULT:
  - All AI Practice attempts are preserved
  - Student can see all attempts in History screen
  - ProgressScreen shows side-by-side comparison of latest two attempts
  - Timeline bar chart shows score progression over all attempts
  - No data is ever lost or overwritten by a new submission

QUESTION 10
"What are the limitations of your system?"
------------------------------------------------------------------------

LIMITATION 1 — Tajweed evaluation is text-based, not acoustic:
  Our system identifies which Tajweed-sensitive letters (makhraj)
  appeared in words that were mismatched. It does NOT acoustically
  measure Madd elongation, Ghunnah nasalization, or Qalqalah echo.
  Direct acoustic Tajweed evaluation would require specialized models.

LIMITATION 2 — Gemini is a black box:
  We cannot see inside Gemini's neural network. If Gemini makes
  a transcription error (especially for rare or dialectal Arabic
  words), our Expert System will score based on that incorrect
  transcription.

LIMITATION 3 — The 70% gate is not strictly enforced:
  The Official Assessment unlock checks if ANY AI practice
  was completed, not whether the student scored ≥ 70%.

LIMITATION 4 — Extra words are not detected:
  If a student inserts extra words not in the expected verse,
  the Expert System does not explicitly classify them as
  "extra words". They appear as mismatches in adjacent words.

LIMITATION 5 — Arabic normalization is broad:
  Diacritics (harakat) are removed before comparison.
  This means a student who correctly recites "بِسْمِ" and one who
  recites "بسم" are treated identically after normalization.
  This is intentional to avoid penalizing harakat differences,
  but it means the system is more lenient with Arabic precision.

LIMITATION 6 — Acoustic fallback is estimated, not verified:
  When Gemini is unavailable, the acoustic fallback produces
  scores based on signal energy and rhythm — NOT based on actual
  word comparison. The scores are realistic-looking but cannot
  confirm whether the student recited the correct words.
